# Why this is not another time tracker

Hubstaff, the Upwork desktop app, Time Doctor and RescueTime between them have tracked
more working hours than anything else ever built. They are mature, they are widely
deployed, and the thing they measure is not what their users think it is.

GroundTruth implements their metric, reports it on every event, and shows you where it
disagrees with what was on the screen.

---

## What they actually measure

Two signals, and only two.

**Activity percentage.** Split the clock into ten-minute segments. Count the minutes in
which any keyboard or mouse input occurred. Divide. Three minutes out of ten is 30%.

**Application and URL names.** Which window had focus, for how long.

That is the whole measurement. `agent/groundtruth/activity.py` implements the first one
faithfully, because an argument you cannot check is worth nothing.

## Why that was the right answer

It is worth being fair about this, because the metric is not stupid. In 2013 there was no
alternative. Reading a screen meant either shipping pixels to a server, which is
unacceptable, or running a model nobody had on hardware nobody owned. Input was the only
signal that was cheap, continuous, and available on every machine.

It is also a real signal. Input genuinely correlates with doing things. Most of the time
the activity percentage and the screen agree, and GroundTruth says so by staying silent:
the comparison only fires in the two cases below, because a tool that contradicted you
constantly would be an accusation rather than a measurement.

## The two directions it fails

**Quiet work scores zero.** Reading documentation. Reviewing a design. A long debugging
stare. Scripture reading. Thinking about a line of code before writing it. All of it
scores near zero, and the dashboard files it as idle. This is the failure that makes
people resent the tool, because the hours it erases are often the hardest ones.

**Loud waste scores full marks.** Forty minutes pulling a feed is continuous input. It
scores near one hundred and ranks among the most productive time of the day.

One keystroke in sixty seconds scores that minute exactly the same as sixty seconds of
typing. The bar is that low, which is why a mouse jiggler defeats the metric completely
and why an entire product category exists to sell them. When people pay money to defeat a
measurement, the measurement has stopped measuring the thing and started measuring
compliance.

## The deeper difference is who it is for

Activity tracking grew up as something an employer installs on a contractor's machine.
Everything follows from that. Screenshots are uploaded on a schedule so somebody else can
look at them. The score exists to be audited. The design question was always "how do we
prove to a third party that work happened", and the answer had to be something a third
party could inspect.

GroundTruth answers a different question: **what did I actually do today?** Nobody else is
the audience. That single change in who is asking is what makes the architecture possible:

- Frames are classified by open weights on infrastructure the operator controls, and
  sending one to a vendor-managed endpoint raises rather than succeeding. There is no
  manager to show them to, so they never need to leave.
- Only derived labels are persisted, scrubbed of credential-shaped text on the way in.
- The weekly review sees category names and durations, never a frame, a title or a
  transcript.
- When the model cannot tell, it says so and asks you, rather than scoring you. An
  employer-facing tool cannot afford to abstain. A tool that works for you can.

## What it does instead

Both numbers, on every event, at every tier:

```
Static documentation page
[Input activity 10% over the last 10m. For comparison, an input-activity tracker
 would score this 10% and flag it as idle; the screen shows research.]
```

That line is generated by the product, not written for a pitch. The old metric is computed
honestly from the same signal those tools use, and placed next to what the screen actually
contained.

GroundTruth also knows the difference between an open window and an activity, which input
alone cannot establish. A focused editor on a machine set never to sleep is recorded as
`unattended`, not as deep work, because nothing was typed and nothing moved on screen. See
`presence.py`.

## What they still do better

A positioning document that claims a clean sweep is not credible, and these are real:

- **It runs anywhere.** No GPU, no model, no inference cost, no cold starts. Activity
  percentage works on a ten-year-old laptop over a bad connection.
- **It is defensible for billing.** An hour backed by input counts and screenshots is
  evidence a client will accept. A semantic label is an opinion, however well evidenced,
  and GroundTruth is not trying to settle invoices.
- **It has no privacy question to answer**, because it never claimed to understand
  anything. A screenshot uploaded to a manager is at least an honest bargain about what is
  being given up.
- **It is mature.** Integrations, payroll, team rollups, a decade of edge cases.

GroundTruth is not a replacement for a contractor billing tool. It is the answer to a
question those tools were never asking.

## The one-line version

> Every tracker ever built answers "were your hands moving?" and lets you read it as
> "were you working?". GroundTruth reads the screen instead, tells you when it cannot
> tell, and never shows anyone else.
